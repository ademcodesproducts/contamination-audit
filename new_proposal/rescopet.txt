This literature review changes the calculus significantly — it surfaces the two most-cited open problems in the field, and neither is well-addressed by the "black-box CoT classifier" scope I proposed last time. Let me rescope around them directly.

The two facts that should drive the pivot
Membership-style detection is fundamentally fragile. Black-box MIA sits at AUC≈0.5 (coin flip) in rigorous comparisons. Grey-box (logit-based) methods look better but are contested. And critically — detection can be actively evaded: paraphrase-laundered contamination (rephrase the benchmark item, train on that) beats n-gram decontamination completely, to the point where a fine-tuned 13B Llama matched GPT-4 on rephrased-trained benchmarks while staying invisible to string-matching filters. There's a paper literally titled "Evading Data Contamination Detection for Language Models is (too) Easy."
Real-world decontamination pipelines are still primitive. Apertus, OLMo 3, Meditron — all still lean on n-gram/token-overlap matching with light semantic bolt-ons. The field knows this is inadequate (see #1) but hasn't shipped anything better into practice.

Put those together and you get the actual frontier gap: every existing detection method is asking "was this string (or a near-paraphrase of it) present in training?" — a membership question that's provably evadable by laundering the surface form. Your DiD/behavioral framework asks a fundamentally different question: "does the model's behavior on this item show the causal fingerprint of shortcut reliance?" That's a capability question, not a membership question — and a contaminator can't launder away the capability boost without also eliminating the reason they contaminated in the first place. That asymmetry is your paper.

Rescoped project

Working title: Behavioral Detection Resists Evasion: A Controlled Causal Study of Contamination Laundering

Core claim to establish: Membership-based contamination detectors (n-gram, embedding similarity, Min-K%, LLM-judge) degrade toward chance as contamination is increasingly paraphrase-laundered — but causal behavioral detection (your DiD/perturbation-brittleness framework) continues to detect the capability inflation, because it measures the downstream effect rather than the upstream string match.

This is a controlled injection study, not an observational audit — which is the single biggest upgrade over your current paper. Right now your DiD is underpowered and observational (you don't actually know ground-truth contamination status with certainty, just pipeline-inferred labels). A controlled study where you inject the contamination gives you ground truth, eliminates the "treatment assignment noise" limitation you already flagged, and lets you sweep laundering strength as an independent variable.

Experimental design
Curate a clean, verified-uncontaminated problem set (math, possibly + code as a stretch) — held out, checked against known corpora.
Inject contamination at graded laundering strengths into SFT training data for open base models (7-8B, matches your existing infra):
Verbatim (positive control — everything should detect this)
Light paraphrase (surface rewording, same structure)
Heavy semantic paraphrase (full rewrite, same underlying problem)
Numeric-perturbation-laundered (change surface numbers, same solution schema — directly tests whether your own perturbation-based detector "sees through" the exact laundering technique it's designed to be robust to)
LoRA fine-tune across these conditions (you've done this exact workflow at Bitmovin) — this is the "expensive but gold standard" causal design ConTAM flagged as rare in the field; you can afford it at small scale where big labs can't at pretraining scale.
Apply the full standard detector suite post-hoc: n-gram overlap, embedding similarity, Min-K%, LLM-as-judge — measure detection AUC as a function of laundering strength. This replicates the known evasion result under controlled conditions (novel: nobody has cleanly shown the AUC-vs-laundering-strength curve under controlled injection before, only observationally or anecdotally).
Apply your DiD/CoT-brittleness framework to the same models — test whether the causal behavioral signal survives laundering where membership detection collapses.
Stretch goal: repeat a subset under GRPO/RL post-training instead of SFT, directly engaging Kocyigit & Yildirim's finding that SFT and GRPO contaminate differently — this is what would push the paper from "solid ICLR" to "field-defining," but I'd scope it as a stretch, not core, given one semester.
Why this is stronger than my previous scope
It directly rebuts/extends a specific, well-known paper ("Evading Detection... is Too Easy") rather than positioning against a vague field gap — reviewers can locate exactly what you're arguing against.
It's a controlled causal experiment, which is rarer and more valued than another observational audit — you're generating ground-truth data, not just measuring against imperfect pipeline labels.
It produces a clean, single quantitative headline result: an "evasion gap" curve — detection AUC (membership methods) vs. laundering strength, overlaid against your causal detection AUC staying flat. That's a one-figure paper hook, which reviewers respond to.
It naturally produces a reusable artifact — the laundering-injection pipeline plus labeled model checkpoints — which is exactly what a Datasets & Benchmarks track fallback wants if the main-track bar isn't cleared.
